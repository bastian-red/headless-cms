Footnotes are an old tool for a real need: a remark that belongs with a sentence but would derail it.[^1] On the web they are often done badly.

## The two taps

A good footnote is one tap away and one tap back. The reference in the text links to the note, and the note links back to the exact place the reader left. Most implementations get the first half right and forget the second, leaving the reader at the bottom of the page hunting for where they were.

## Numbers that make sense

Notes are numbered in the order they appear, and each number is a real link with a name that makes sense out of context. A screen reader announcing "link, 1" is not helpful; "footnote 1" is.

## Keep them short

A footnote longer than the paragraph it hangs from is an essay in the wrong place.[^2] Long asides deserve their own section, or their own post.

[^1]: Book designers have argued about footnotes against endnotes for centuries. The colophon of almost any scholarly edition will tell you which side its designer took.
