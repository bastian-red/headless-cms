The same post can be reached at more than one address. With and without a trailing slash. With a tracking parameter from a newsletter. Through the slug it had before an editor renamed it. To a reader these are the same page. To a search engine, unless we say otherwise, they are several pages with identical text.

## Name the one that counts

Every post page names its canonical address in its head. Search engines then treat the other addresses as copies and credit the canonical one with every link pointing at any of them.

## Redirect what you can

A canonical tag is a hint. A redirect is a fact. Old slugs answer with a permanent redirect to the current one, so a link from three years ago lands on the right page and the address bar shows its real name. Trailing slashes are normalised the same way.

## Leave the rest alone

Query parameters we do not use are ignored by the page and left out of the canonical address. We do not redirect them away, because a reader's analytics tool may want them, and the canonical tag already tells search engines what to do.
